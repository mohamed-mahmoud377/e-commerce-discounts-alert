# System review, 25 October 2026

## Verdict

The system is healthy and doing its job: 43,463 products tracked, every slice crawled within its interval,
no failing jobs, no crashes, and every Telegram message delivered. The weak points are not breakage but
focus and data quality: half the crawl goes to low-value kitchen gadgets, two "evidence" signals are too
common on Amazon to mean much, and Amazon's featured-seller switching can fake price moves the same way
noon's sellers did.

This review is based on the first 1.5 hours of production data (25 October 2026, 20:08 to 21:35 UTC), so it
covers crawling and data quality well, but not yet the history-based deal alerts, which start after 3 days.
Re-run it after a week.

## Problems found

| # | Problem | Evidence | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Kitchen appliances eat half the crawl | 22,113 of 43,463 products (51%): 12,168 on noon, 9,945 on Amazon, mostly blenders, irons and kettles above 700 EGP | TVs, fridges and phones are revisited less often than they could be |
| 2 | "Only N left" is meaningless on Amazon | 44.7% of Amazon products show 3 or fewer left (49% show 10 or fewer) | It still counts as evidence for new-listing alerts on Amazon, so those alerts are weaker than they look |
| 3 | Amazon's featured seller can switch | Kenwood fryer 5,999 to 8,893 EGP (+48%) and air fryer 5,999 to 7,499 (+25%) within an hour: likely a different seller winning the listing | When it switches back, a long-held high price could look like a real drop. Same class of issue as noon's sellers, milder (2 of 90 moves) |
| 4 | Several listings per product on noon | 1,466 noon titles appear more than once (different sellers or colours) | One real deal can produce several alerts, as with the four Nespresso colours |
| 5 | Every alert so far is a low-confidence "new listing" | 25 of 25 | Expected for now; the history-based rules start after 3 days |
| 6 | The Mac is the only noon worker | noon is refused from the droplet | When the Mac sleeps, noon stops (you get a "stalled" warning) |
| 7 | Your Mac's disk is 94% full | about 28 GB free | Not this project's fault; could stop the worker's log or the Mac itself |
| 8 | Tokens travel over plain HTTP | Worker calls and the dashboard link carry a token; `/docs` (API schema, no data) is public | Low risk for a personal tool, but anyone on the path could read a token |
| 9 | Deploys leave a short gap | "could not report to coordinator" lines when the worker's release message hits the restarting server | That job waits up to 5 minutes for its lease to expire; harmless |
| 10 | 1,403 Amazon products (8%) show no price | Unavailable items still listed in categories | Small wasted space; they never alert |

## Improvements, ranked

| Rank | Change | Fixes | Effort | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Narrow the kitchen group (**done** 2026-10-26: minimum 2,000 EGP, every 180 min; 22,198 → 13,829 products; TVs, fridges, ACs, phones, laptops, monitors start at every 20 min) | #1 | 10 min, config only | Raise its `min_price` to about 1,500 EGP, or replace the whole small-appliances root with the sub-categories you care about (air fryers, coffee machines, microwaves, blenders) |
| 2 | Stop counting "only N left" as evidence on Amazon (**done** 2026-10-26) | #2 | 30 min | In `pricing.evaluate()`, count `only_left` only for noon (pass the site in), with a test |
| 3 | Confirm before alerting | #3, #4, and fake drops in general | Half a day | Before sending a price-error or new-listing alert, fetch the product page once to read the buy-box seller, condition and price; skip if it disagrees |
| 4 | One alert per product, not per seller or colour | #4 | Half a day | Group listings by brand + normalised title (or model number) and keep only the best-scoring alert per group within 24 h |
| 5 | Guard against seller switches on Amazon | #3 | 1 hour | In `compute_stats()`, ignore price levels that held under 6 hours when computing the typical price and the all-time high, so a short switch cannot inflate the reference |
| 6 | A second home worker | #6 | 1 hour | A Raspberry Pi or old laptop at home (or a friend's PC) running `deals worker`; nothing to change in code |
| 7 | Private network for workers | #8 | 1 hour | Tailscale on the droplet and each device; close port 8787 to the internet; point workers at the Tailscale address |
| 8 | Tune thresholds after a week of history | #5 | 15 min weekly | Look at the alerts you found useful; adjust `min_drop` per group |
| 9 | Release jobs before restarting the coordinator | #9 | 30 min | In `deploy.sh`, restart the worker first, or retry `release` for a few seconds |
| 10 | Skip unavailable products in stats views | #10 | Optional | Already true for alerts |
